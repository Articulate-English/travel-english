Beach & Leisure

Travel vocabulary for Italian speakers · B1 + B2 level

Key vocabulary (click for Italian definition):

sun lounger · beach towel · shoreline walk · snorkel gear · deck chair · water sports · lifeguard tower · seaside promenade

Questions · Answer in full sentences:

What should you pack for a beach holiday?

Pack swimwear, sun cream, a hat, sunglasses, beach towel, flip-flops, water shoes and a reusable water bottle. Check local weather and dress codes for the beach.

Rearrange the words:

[I / would / like / to / rent / a / sun / lounger / and / an / umbrella]

I would like to rent a sun lounger and an umbrella.

Describe the picture:

A sandy beach with rows of sun loungers and parasols. Families and couples relax near the shoreline while lifeguards watch from a tower. The water is calm and turquoise.

Questions · Your experience:

Have you ever been sunburned on holiday? What happened?

I forgot sun cream on a boat trip and burned my shoulders. I now apply SPF 50 every two hours and wear a hat. Prevention is easier than treatment.

Compare the pictures:

Left is a calm, family-friendly beach with loungers and parasols. Right is a wilder, rocky coast with waves and fewer facilities. The first suits relaxation, the second suits adventure.

Deeper questions:

How can beaches be made safer for tourists?

Clear flag systems, lifeguard stations, rip-current warnings, first-aid points and multilingual signage reduce accidents. Educating visitors about tides and currents is essential.

Speaking · 2 minutes:

Describe your ideal beach holiday. Where would you go and why?

My ideal beach holiday is a quiet cove in southern Italy with clear water and local food. I would snorkel in the morning, read under a parasol at noon and enjoy seafood at sunset. Avoiding crowds matters most.

Vocabulary review - B2+ words:

sunburn · rip current · high tide · sun cream · lifeguard · beach vendor · parasol · flip-flops

Grammar · Passive voice:

Sun loungers are rented at the beach.
Towels are provided by hotels.
Warnings are posted by lifeguards.

Swimming is prohibited after dark. Equipment must be returned by 6 pm. Fines are charged for littering.

Useful expressions:

"Can I rent a parasol?" · "Is the water safe for swimming?" · "Where is the nearest lifeguard?" · "What time does the beach close?"

Cultural tip:

In Italy, many beaches are "stabilimenti balneari" with paid access, loungers and bars. Free public beaches exist but are busier. Greet staff with "Buongiorno" and respect local rules about noise and litter.
